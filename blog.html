A small paper for very big thoughts

The Day We Stopped Asking “Why”

A quiet investigation into grown-ups, silly questions, lost sparks, and the strange beauty of looking up.

Written for curious humans Thursday Edition The Wonder Project

Once, we asked questions all day long. We asked why the Moon followed us home, whether fish became thirsty, and where darkness went when the light came on.

Then, very slowly, many of us became quiet.

Not because we knew everything. Not because the world became less strange. We became quiet because we learned that questions could make other people laugh.

IMPORTANT NOTICE: Curiosity has no age limit.

Today’s Question

When did looking at the Moon become “childish”?

The Moon did not become less beautiful when you grew older. You simply became busier.

BREAKING NEWS

Millions of Questions Have Gone Missing

The Invisible Rule

Imagine that you are sitting in a classroom. You raise your hand. Your heart beats a little faster. You ask a question.

“Why is the sky blue?”

Someone laughs. Another person rolls their eyes. A voice whispers, “Everybody knows that.”

You laugh too, even though it hurts. Then you make a small promise: I will not ask again.

Nobody hears that promise. But sometimes it stays for years.

Questions Are Requests for Knowledge

Every honest question means, “Please help me understand.”

A question may sound mixed-up. It may use the wrong words. It may come from someone who does not yet know what you know.

That does not make the person foolish. It means they are standing at the beginning of learning.

“Do you realize that when you belittle people for asking ‘stupid’ questions, you destroy their ability to keep asking questions?”
NO SILLY QUESTIONS

The Smallest Dream Killer

People often think dreams disappear because life becomes difficult. Sometimes they do. But sometimes a dream disappears because someone was made to feel foolish for wondering.

One laugh can make a person hide their next question. One eye roll can make them stop sharing ideas. One rude answer can make them believe that curiosity is something embarrassing.

That is how a bright, wondering person can slowly become someone who says, “Never mind.”

A question is not proof that you know less. It is proof that you are trying to grow.

THE SPARK REPAIR SHOP

How to Begin Wondering Again

STEP 1

Notice One Thing

Look at something ordinary: a spoon, a shadow, a shoelace, a puddle, or the sound of a door closing.

STEP 2

Ask a Tiny Question

Why is it shaped that way? Who made it first? What would happen if it disappeared?

STEP 3

Do Not Rush the Answer

Let the question stay with you for a while. Wondering is not a race. Spend some time thinking about it rather that going around and 'googling it'!

STEP 4

Protect Other People’s Questions

Instead of laughing, say, “That is interesting. Let us think about it.”

A Note About the Moon

The Moon Does Not Care How Old You Are

The Moon never asks whether you are too old to admire it. Clouds do not float only for children. Rain does not sound less magical because you have a job, bills, or a busy calendar.

Wonder did not leave you. It may simply be waiting for you to look up.
