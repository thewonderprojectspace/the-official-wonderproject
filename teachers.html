Before the next bell

Teaching can be meaningful and exhausting at the same time. One truth does not cancel the other.

Behind every lesson plan is a person carrying preparation, assessment, behaviour, communication, administration, changing expectations and a life outside school.

This space is not going to tell you to simply “be more organised”. It is here to help you notice what is heavy, what is working, what you need, and what should perhaps never have been normalised in the first place.

A field note for difficult weeks

You are not failing because teaching feels hard.

Some weeks ask for too much. Some classes do not respond the way you hoped. Some systems are clumsy. Some days you will question yourself. That does not erase the care, skill and thinking you bring to the room.

A difficult class is not a verdict on your ability. A brilliant lesson is not the only evidence that you are growing.

Teaching has seasons too

You do not have to feel the same way about your work every week.

☼

The bright weeks

Things click. Students respond. You remember why you chose this work.

Notice what created this energy. It may be worth protecting.
◒

The stretched weeks

There is simply too much. Nothing is catastrophic, but your capacity is being consumed.

Ask what can be simplified, delayed, shared or done imperfectly.
☁

The hard weeks

A class, interaction, workload or personal season makes everything feel harder.

You do not need to turn every difficult week into a character-building exercise.
↗

The rebuilding weeks

You try something different. A boundary changes. A routine improves. Energy slowly returns.

Progress in teaching is often procedural before it is inspirational.
