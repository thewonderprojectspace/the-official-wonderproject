basically... the actual story.
SAMPLE STORY 001

I thought everyone was ahead of me.

Then I actually talked to them.

Turns out one had changed degrees. One had failed two subjects. One had no idea what they wanted either.

I spent almost a year looking at people around me and assuming everyone had some secret plan.

They looked confident. They had internships. They seemed busy.

So obviously my brain concluded: everyone has figured out life except me.

Eventually I started talking to people properly.

One had changed degrees twice. Another had failed subjects. Someone else admitted they had no idea what they wanted after graduation.

Nothing changed about my life that day.

I just stopped treating other people's outside appearance as evidence against myself.

THE THOUGHT I KEPT “You know their update. Not their whole story.”
SAMPLE STORY 002

I failed.

Like... properly failed.

I thought the result had confirmed every bad thing I had quietly believed about myself.

When I saw the mark, I didn't immediately become resilient.

I cried. Avoided everyone. Ate something questionable at 1am.

Very inspiring.

A few days later I wrote down everything that had actually gone wrong.

I hadn't understood two major topics. I started studying too late. I was exhausted.

Those were problems.

“I am stupid” wasn't one of them.

THE THOUGHT I ACTED ON “Describe the problem without becoming the problem.”
SAMPLE STORY 003

Life happened.

University stopped being the biggest thing.

There was stuff happening at home and suddenly assignments felt incredibly unimportant.

I kept comparing myself to the version of me that existed before everything got difficult.

That person studied more. Exercised more. replied to emails.

Very annoying person, honestly.

Eventually someone told me:

“Stop expecting your normal output from an abnormal season.”

I didn't suddenly feel great.

But I stopped treating survival like poor productivity.

THE THOUGHT I KEPT “Different season. Different expectations.”
TINY TURNING POINT 004

I couldn't start.

So I made the goal embarrassingly small.

“Study for three hours” became “open the document.”

I was behind on everything.

So naturally I responded by creating an enormous productivity plan that made me want to do absolutely nothing.

Eventually my rule became:

open the file.

That was the entire task.

Sometimes I stopped there. Usually I didn't.

Starting tiny didn't magically make me disciplined.

It just removed the argument I kept having with myself before beginning.

THE THOUGHT I ACTED ON “Make starting easier than avoiding.”
SAMPLE STORY 005

My dream changed.

I thought that meant I had wasted years.

I had spent so long telling everyone what I wanted to become that changing direction felt embarrassing.

I kept going because I didn't want to explain why I had changed my mind.

Eventually I realised I was choosing years of the wrong thing to avoid one awkward conversation.

That felt slightly ridiculous.

The years weren't wasted.

They were how I learned what I didn't want.

THE THOUGHT I KEPT “Changing direction is still movement.”
SMALL WIN 006

Nothing dramatic happened.

I just had a good Tuesday.

I went to class. Ate properly. Finished one task. Called a friend.

For months I kept waiting for some huge turning point.

A new opportunity. A perfect routine. Sudden motivation.

It never arrived.

Instead there was one ordinary Tuesday where things felt slightly easier.

Then another.

Apparently life improvements have terrible marketing.

THE THOUGHT I KEPT “A better life may begin as a better afternoon.”
