01 Why is the sky blue? +
☁️

The answer

Sunlight contains many colours. When it enters Earth's atmosphere, tiny molecules scatter shorter blue wavelengths more strongly than most other colours. That scattered blue light reaches our eyes from every direction.

Keep wondering: Why does the sky sometimes become orange, pink or red?

02 Do animals dream? +
🐶

The answer

Many animals experience sleep stages similar to humans, including rapid eye movement sleep. Scientists have observed behaviours suggesting that animals such as dogs, cats, rats and birds may dream.

Keep wondering: What might your pet be dreaming about?

03 Why do we forget dreams so quickly? +
💭

The answer

During sleep, the brain does not always store dreams in the same way it stores waking memories. Unless we wake during or soon after a dream and think about it, the memory can disappear very quickly.

Keep wondering: Could writing dreams down help us remember them?

04 Can plants recognise each other? +
🌱

The answer

Plants do not recognise others as humans do, but they respond to chemical signals, light, touch, competition and nearby organisms. Some plants even behave differently around genetically related plants.

Keep wondering: Can plants warn one another about danger?

05 Why does time feel faster when we are having fun? +
⏰

The answer

When we are deeply focused or enjoying ourselves, we pay less attention to time passing. During boredom or discomfort, we notice each moment more closely, which can make time seem slower.

Keep wondering: Does time feel different depending on our emotions?

06 If space is silent, how do astronauts communicate? +
🚀

The answer

Sound needs matter, such as air, to travel. Space has almost no matter, so ordinary sound cannot move through it. Astronauts use radios, which send electromagnetic waves that can travel through space.

Keep wondering: What would space sound like if it contained air?

07 Why do we forget why we walked into a room? +
🚪

The answer

Your brain is a brilliant organiser. When you walk through a doorway, it may treat the change of space like the beginning of a new chapter and update what it is focusing on. Scientists sometimes call this the doorway effect.

Retracing your steps can restore the earlier context and help your brain find the thought again.

Try this: Before turning around, recall what you were doing immediately before you crossed the doorway.

08 Where do ants go when it rains? +
🐜

The answer

Most ants return to underground nests. Their tunnels and chambers can help drain water, and some chambers sit high enough to remain dry. Ants may also sense changes in humidity and air pressure before heavy rain.

Some ants have remarkable emergency plans. Fire ants can link their bodies into floating rafts during floods.

Look closely: Before the first drops fall, can you spot ants moving faster than usual?

09 What do bees do when it is raining? +
🐝

The answer

Rain is dangerous for bees because large drops can knock them from the air. Bees usually remain in their hive, while a bee caught outside may shelter beneath a flower or leaf until it is safe to fly again.

Inside the hive, bees seal small gaps with propolis and work together to regulate temperature and humidity.

Keep wondering: How do thousands of bees coordinate to protect one tiny home?
