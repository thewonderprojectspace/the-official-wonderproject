Five carriages

Stories from the
exhausting middle.

These stories are not about arriving. They are about the strange courage required to remain in motion when the destination cannot yet be seen.

Carriage 01

Too far to feel like a beginner On losing the excitement that made starting easier

+

The beginning had its own energy. Everything was new enough to feel meaningful. Small improvements felt enormous because I had only just started.

Then the novelty disappeared. The work became ordinary, repetitive and heavier than the dream had looked from a distance. I was no longer new enough to celebrate every step, but not advanced enough to feel confident.

This was the part nobody had described: the long middle where effort became routine and routine stopped feeling like evidence.

I began to understand that commitment starts where novelty ends. The quiet repetition was not a less meaningful part of the journey. It was the journey becoming real.

The beginning did not disappear. It became the distance behind me.

Carriage 02

Watching other trains pass On comparison and the fear of being left behind

+

Other people’s progress became clearest when my own felt uncertain. I saw their announcements, achievements, finished work and destinations. I saw none of the tunnels they had crossed.

Their speed made mine feel like failure. I forgot that we were not travelling on the same track, carrying the same weight or heading towards the same place.

Comparison made every step feel late. It turned somebody else’s arrival into evidence that I had missed my chance.

But another train passing did not mean mine had stopped. Speed was not the only measure of movement, and timing was not a hierarchy of human worth.

Their arrival was not proof of my failure. It was simply a different journey becoming visible.

Carriage 03

Carrying several destinations On holding more than one dream without apologising

+

I carried more than one dream, and each one demanded time, attention and belief. People often spoke as though seriousness required me to choose only one version of myself.

I wondered whether wanting to learn, create, lead, train and build meant that I lacked focus. Some days, the weight of carrying several futures made every direction feel incomplete.

Eventually I stopped treating my complexity as a defect. Not every dream had to grow at the same speed. Some could rest without being abandoned.

A meaningful life did not need to become a single straight line. It needed an honest rhythm: knowing what required attention now and what could wait without disappearing.

I was not lost because I contained more than one possible destination.

Carriage 04

Resting without leaving the train On burnout and confusing exhaustion with failure

+

I treated exhaustion as proof that I was not disciplined enough. When my body asked for rest, I answered with guilt. When my attention weakened, I demanded more concentration.

I believed that stopping for a moment would undo all the distance I had travelled. Rest felt dangerously similar to giving up.

But the train did not require me to run through every carriage to prove I wanted to arrive. Sometimes I could sit, breathe and let the movement carry me.

Rest was not the opposite of progress. It was how I remained capable of continuing a journey longer than one burst of motivation.

I could pause inside the journey without returning to the station.

Carriage 05

Continuing without visible light On trusting movement before the destination appears

+

I kept looking ahead for reassurance. I wanted one clear sign that the work would matter, that the waiting would end and that I was travelling towards something real.

The tunnel offered no such certainty. It gave me only small evidence: a passing light, the sound of wheels, the stations already behind me and the fact that the view outside continued changing.

I had confused visibility with existence. The exit could exist beyond a curve even when my current position made it impossible to see.

So I continued—not because I knew exactly where the light was, but because I had already become someone who could travel through the dark.

I could not see the end of the tunnel, but I was no longer standing where I had begun.
