A NOTE FOR THE PERSON STILL BECOMING

Dear student

You are allowed to begin before you feel ready.

You finished school, found a field that pulled at your curiosity and started imagining another degree, another skill or a life you have not quite told anyone about yet. You may not be sure. That is all right. Doing meaningful things before you feel ready is often how you discover where you want to go.

At first, some of it will be boring. You will doubt yourself. You may think, “I cannot do this. I am not made for this.” People may tell you that you are missing out or that you should be enjoying this age differently. But they cannot see the quiet dreams taking shape inside you.

Write the dream down. Give it a form. Then give that form time.

Choose a minimum starting point and a minimum finishing point: the small promise you will keep even when the day, the world or your life becomes messy. Small steps are not a lesser version of progress. They are how the larger goal becomes possible. Take it one day at a time and trust the process—there is no shortcut around becoming.

Thank the people who transform you. Learn from the people who doubt you. Hold close the people who love you. The work ahead will introduce you to a version of yourself that only you will fully understand.

We at Jigyasa Verse believe in you and in the dreams you are still learning how to name. We will be beside you while you build them.

Do not spend your whole life watching the game.

Step into the arena.

When you attempt the work yourself, you begin to understand what it costs just to arrive: the repetition, failure, discipline and courage nobody sees. You become slower to criticise the people performing for your leisure—because you finally recognise the human effort behind the result.

A note for professors and educators

Thank you for teaching beyond the edge of what is known.

You welcome learners into fields that took you years to understand. You translate specialised language without removing its depth. You answer the thoughtful question, the anxious question and sometimes the same question four different ways.

You create room for students who arrive with different schooling, languages, confidence, finances, responsibilities and ways of thinking. You supervise unseen drafts, write feedback late into the evening and lend belief to students before they have learned to lend it to themselves.

Your work is not only the transfer of information. You show learners how a discipline thinks, how evidence changes a mind and how intellectual humility can coexist with expertise. Thank you for helping the next generation cross the bridge.

May this space make that work a little lighter.
