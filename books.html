Archive of real lives · no heroic editing

Things we learned while becoming.

These first pieces come from the founder’s notebook. Community stories will be added only with the writer’s permission.

This archive will never invent a contributor or call fiction a true story. Empty space is more honest than a voice that did not consent to be here.

From the founder’s notebook

There are seasons when your life is changing but nothing around you appears impressed. The work happens in private: another early morning, another page rewritten, another attempt that looks too small to count.

I used to think progress had to become visible before it became real. Life taught me the opposite. Roots are not less important because the world cannot applaud them. Some of the most honest becoming happens before there is evidence anyone else can understand.

— from the beginning of JigyasaVerse

From the founder’s notebook

People often ask for the simplest version of you. One ambition. One direction. One sentence that makes your life easy to introduce. But curiosity rarely grows in a straight line, and neither do people.

I have learned that being interested in many things is not the same as being committed to nothing. Sometimes the thread connecting everything is invisible from the outside. You are still allowed to follow it.

— a note for the difficult introductions

From the founder’s notebook

Not every question disappears because it was answered. Some disappear because the room made them embarrassing. We learn to ask for less, to sound certain, and to hide the strange thought that might reveal how unfinished we still are.

JigyasaVerse began as a refusal to keep doing that. Wonder does not need to justify itself before it enters the room. A question can be clumsy, inconvenient, or years too early—and still deserve somewhere safe to exist.

— why this universe exists
